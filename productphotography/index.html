Overview

Images that show what the product is actually made of.

Two routes, one standard: a classic studio shoot and 3D visualisation. We pick whichever gives the better result for your product, your deadline and your budget — or combine both inside a single catalogue, so nothing drifts apart between them.

Either way the product comes out with the same set: catalogue shot, material detail, in-context frame and colourways. No follow-ups, no second shoot when one frame turns out to be missing.

01 — Scope

One set of material for the entire product page

A

Catalogue packshot

White or neutral background, gradient built with light. The same product at 3/4, in profile, from above and from behind — consistent across the whole series.

B

Detail and macro

What the catalogue shot doesn't show: the seam, the weave, leather grain, grain direction, the way lacquer catches light on an edge.

C

Variants and colourways

A dozen finishes of the same model, lit identically. The client compares the product, not the difference between shooting days.

D

Lifestyle and context

The product in a scene: interior, hand, countertop, street. A frame that sells a mood rather than only describing an object.

E

Channel-ready crops

One image ships as 1:1 for the product page, 4:5 for social and a horizontal frame for banners. No last-minute cropping.

F

Retouching and files

Clean-up, colour, cut-out backgrounds as PNG, sRGB for web and CMYK for print, named for your store import.

02 — Route A · Photography

When real material and texture are the point

The product physically exists and has a sheen, a weave and an irregularity worth showing. A studio shoot delivers that fastest: light set for one specific material, controlled shadow, colour matched to the sample.

03 — Route B · CGI

When the product doesn't exist yet, or there are too many variants

Camera, lens and light are scene parameters, not another shooting day. The frame changes in a minute and the product stays identical — no drift in colour, perspective or shadow. On a catalogue counted in dozens of items, that is the difference between a day and a week of work.

06 — FAQ

The questions that come up most

How much does a product shoot cost?

It depends on the number of products, the number of frames per product and whether lifestyle shots are needed. After a short brief you get a range the same day, and a firm quote once the shot list is agreed.

Can photography and CGI live in one catalogue?

Yes — that is the most common scenario. Products available physically go in front of the lens; new releases and colourways are built in 3D. The key is agreeing shared lighting and background up front, so the product page stays uniform.

What do I need to provide?

For photography: the products themselves and any colour references. For CGI: a CAD model or technical drawings with dimensions, material samples or photographs of them, and the list of finishes.

How are image rights handled?

By default you receive a licence for owned and paid channels with no time limit. A full transfer of rights is possible and is quoted separately.

How long does it take?

A typical series is 5–10 working days from test approval to delivered files. On larger catalogues we work in batches, so the first products reach the store before the whole series is finished.
